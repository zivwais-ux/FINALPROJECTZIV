# Harbor & Vale Retention Playbook

> Synthetic internal document written for this course project. Harbor & Vale and every
> name, policy and figure below are fictional.

Owner: Customer Retention team. Applies to all customers of the Harbor & Vale online store.

## Who we contact first
Retention outreach is ranked by the churn model's risk score. Contact customers in this order:
1. Customers with a risk score of 0.70 or higher, newest first.
2. Customers who filed a complaint in the last month, whatever their score.
3. Customers with a risk score between 0.40 and 0.70.
Customers below 0.40 receive no individual outreach; they stay in the regular newsletter.

## New customers (tenure 0 to 1 months)
New customers churn far more often than established ones. Every new customer flagged by the
model gets a welcome call within 3 days of the flag, plus a first-order follow-up email asking
whether delivery and payment went smoothly. Do not offer a discount on the first contact:
first fix the experience, then offer an incentive if the customer is still at risk a week later.

## Customers who complained
A complaint in the last month is the strongest single warning sign we track. The rule is
"resolve before you reward": a support agent closes the complaint first, and only then may the
retention team offer a coupon. A coupon sent while the complaint is still open is treated as an
apology for nothing and usually increases churn.

## Cash on delivery (COD) customers
COD customers cannot be reached with wallet cashback, because they pay at the door. For them
use free-delivery vouchers or a small discount printed on the next invoice. Encourage, but never
require, a switch to UPI or a card.

## Channels
- High risk (0.70 and above): phone call by a retention agent.
- Medium risk (0.40 to 0.70): app push notification, then email after 2 days without response.
- Never contact the same customer more than twice in 14 days.

## What we never do
- Never use the churn score in a message to the customer.
- Never treat two spellings of a category (for example "COD" and "Cash on Delivery") as the same
  segment in a campaign without checking with the data team: they behave differently.
- Never act on a model score when the latest pipeline validation report says FAILED.
